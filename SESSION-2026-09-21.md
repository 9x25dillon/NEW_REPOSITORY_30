# Session review — 2026-09-21

We moved SONIC DRIFTER toward a game about growing an organism, maintaining
useful places and bonding with creatures. The user identified the underlying
problem: later worlds lose interest. More mechanics are now available, but
whether they sustain interest is still a playtest question.

This review separates observed work from proposed improvements. It does not
claim to measure time saved by a different prompt or player enjoyment.

## What we delivered

- Buildable mitochondria store energy for later stamina recovery. Holding X
  grows one on an existing structure; a short X press places a cell on release.
  The cost is the selected cell and the next cell, previewed in the HUD.
- A weakened boss can be tamed by holding Y nearby. Bonding keeps it alive,
  opens the next world and recruits a companion. Killing remains an option.
- Strider, Warden and Weaver bosses have different pursuit or volley behavior
  and different companion benefits. Companion forms change the player's
  appearance. Companions persist within a run, with three bounded ranks.
- Limb tips now provide dash recovery, help with bonding or intercept volleys.
  Their role depends on the tip cell; support respects depth.
- Ribbons and Sentinels add circling and ambush behavior in later worlds.
- Procedural creature silhouettes, organelle feedback, controller-aware prompts,
  inventory role labels and a reorganized HUD make the systems more visible.
- Both standalone HTML variants, regression tests, browser interaction checks
  and a play guide were updated. The research physics library was not changed.

## Key decisions and their costs

| Decision | Reason | Tradeoff to revisit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Use standard Xbox controls | The user usually plays on an Elite Series 2 | Hardware feel and paddle mappings were not tested |
| Make X distinguish tap and hold | Add organelles without requiring another button | Placement happens on release; this can feel less immediate |
| Make Y offer a bond during combat | Reuse the existing throne control in a different phase | The player must learn a third contextual use of Y |
| Rest the player's weapons during a bond attempt | Prevent accidental kills while deliberately taming | Bosses still attack; the timing may be demanding |
| Convert organelle hosts into utility structures | Gripping to recover stamina must not destroy the station | The player gives up a gun and spends two cells |
| Give limbs defensive and support roles | Useful appendages should not repeat the failed farming aura | The roles still need evidence that they change build decisions |
| Keep one active companion and cap ranks at three | Prevent unbounded stacking and preserve a choice | Progression can flatten again after the collection is complete |
| Keep new adaptations in game/ | Separate fictional gameplay from the acoustic instrument | New biology-themed features must be described honestly |

The user explicitly chose the direction: controller play, visual improvement,
useful limbs, more creature variety, tameable bosses and more activities such as
mitochondria. The exact costs, distances, timings, rank cap and three-form cycle
were assistant implementation choices, not user-specified balance targets.

## Evidence and limits

The baseline suite passed 333 tests. The upgraded full suite passed 344 tests
with zero failures, taking approximately 427 seconds in this environment.
Subsequent cost-selection and presentation refinements were checked with the
focused ecology/controller tests, type checking, rebuilding and browser checks;
the entire suite was not rerun after every small refinement.

Both TypeScript configurations passed. Chromium passed 14 interaction checks
using simulated standard Xbox input, including tap/hold separation, organelle
cost, pause/resume, companion switching, live-simulation bonding and world
transition. Browser checks reported no rendering exceptions. Screenshots
exposed overlapping HUD elements; the layout was corrected and inspected again.

A deliberately removed organelle resource cost made the ecology tests fail.
The mutation was restored and the focused tests passed afterward. That is
evidence for that regression check, not a claim of comprehensive mutation testing.

The browser scene screenshot is staged. Neither it nor synthetic inputs proves
that a physical Elite 2 feels good, or that the late game is engaging.

## Unresolved assumptions

1. **More activities will reduce repetition.** Three boss forms repeat, and
   companion ranks stop growing. We have not measured engagement after that.
2. **Bonding is practical under pressure.** The 30% threshold, 120-micron reach
   and three-second hold are initial values. Damage interrupts bonding, and
   the damage grace period must expire before progress resumes. A high-damage
   discharge may also kill a boss before a comfortable taming window appears.
3. **Mitochondria create useful routes.** The two-cell cost, storage and transfer
   rates may encourage revisiting stations, or may reward camping instead.
   Lifting or losing the host loses its organelle; that consequence may need
   stronger explanation to players.
4. **Limb roles are worth their construction cost.** Guard tips need anchor
   cells, while sails and resonators are available through other cell types.
   We have not compared useful activations per cell spent across real runs.
5. **The new visuals improve recognition.** Screenshots support layout review,
   not recognition during a dense fight. Thin lines and small text still need
   checks at the user's actual display size.
6. **Input and progression scope match expectations.** No physical paddle,
   wireless connection, cross-session save or accessibility-specific control
   profile was added or validated.

## Three concrete ways Codex could have been more efficient

1. **Bound discovery to the repository.** I ran `find ..` for instructions and
   traversed unrelated home-directory repositories, producing permission errors
   and irrelevant matches. A repository-scoped `rg --files --hidden` plus
   explicit ancestor instruction paths would have answered the actual question.
2. **Stop polling a long suite every few seconds.** I repeatedly checked the
   baseline process and tailed unchanged logs. Its eventual runtime was about
   507 seconds. A scheduled check while reading the game handoff or preparing
   the next independent step would have used that waiting time better.
3. **Verify one complete interaction earlier.** I implemented a broad set of
   changes before the first visual inspection. A shadowed `run` variable failed
   type checking, and screenshots later exposed HUD overlap. A smaller first
   increment—X tap/hold, one organelle, its prompt and a screenshot—would have
   surfaced integration and layout problems before repeating the pattern.

These are observed process problems. Their exact avoidable time cost was not
measured. They are my responsibility; the user should not need to prescribe
tool discipline to get efficient work.

## Three ways the user could make the collaboration more productive

These are opportunities to supply high-value context, not a requirement to
design the whole implementation or write a perfect prompt.

1. **Lead with the experience that needs to change.** The opening request was
   broad; the later message supplied the Elite 2 and the late-game repetition.
   Combining those in the opening would focus discovery immediately. Example:
   “I play on an Xbox Elite 2. Later worlds feel repetitive; make exploration
   and organism building more rewarding.”
2. **Rank the ideas and preserve room for interpretation.** Visuals, limbs,
   enemies, bosses and player variety arrived together; mitochondria came as
   another useful direction. A priority sentence would make the first playable
   increment easier to choose: “Prioritize useful building and boss taming;
   improve visuals enough to explain them. Mitochondria are a suggestion.”
3. **Provide one actual play example and a completion criterion.** No specific
   aeon, boredom timestamp, run report or desired session length was supplied.
   Those observations would help distinguish repetition from pacing or weak
   rewards. A useful template is: “At aeon [actual number], after [actual time],
   I mostly do [action]. I want a reason to choose [alternative]. Here is
   `drifter.report()`. Done when [observable outcome].”

The missing details are observable; the improvement from supplying them is a
reasoned expectation, not a conclusive measurement of user efficiency. The
mid-session suggestion was valid creative steering and was incorporated.

## A reusable next-session prompt

> Read Hand_off.md and the linked play guide. I play SONIC DRIFTER on an Xbox
> Elite 2. Focus this session on whether mitochondria and taming make later
> worlds more interesting. My play observation is: [what happened, aeon, time].
> My report is: [paste drifter.report()]. Prioritize the biggest problem shown
> by that evidence. Keep movement driven by the field and keep gameplay changes
> out of src/. Use your judgment for reversible implementation choices. Finish
> with relevant tests, a rebuilt game, and a clear account of what still needs
> physical-controller playtesting. Ask before expanding the feature scope.

The useful structure is **goal, context, constraints and a completion
criterion**, consistent with [official OpenAI prompting guidance](https://learn.chatgpt.com/guides/best-practices).
The examples above are tailored to this session, not copied product promises.
Mention commit/push authority when publishing is part of the task; a request
for development alone should not imply permission to publish.

## Two vocabulary words

**Affordance:** a property that makes an action possible; interface cues help
the player perceive that possibility. A weakened boss can be bonded with, and
its ring and Y prompt communicate that affordance. Useful prompt: “Make the
bonding affordance obvious before I miss the window.”

**Telemetry:** measurements recorded by a running system. Event counts and
state in `drifter.report()` are telemetry. Useful prompt: “Add telemetry for
failed bond attempts and energy drawn from mitochondria so we can tune from
play evidence.” Those particular measurements are proposals, not yet implemented.
