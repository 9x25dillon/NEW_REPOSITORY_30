## 1. The throne is sated at the fourth helping, and nothing said so

The loudest line in the aeon-6 report:

```
throne: fed [50 helpings] -> 622 awake 4113/5850
events: discharge=100 sovereign-hit=98 devour=40 volley=421 hit:volley=14
diagnosis: 100 DISCHARGES LANDED AND IT LIVED. GET THE KING ONTO A BUILDING
```

Ninety-eight landed discharges, and the king finished on seventy per cent of its
bar. They dodged 407 of 421 volleys. **Nothing they did in that fight was
wrong,** and the game's answer to it was a tip about where to stand.

The run was decided at the throne. Measured against `worldFrom` and its
neighbours, every quantity feeding buys is clamped:

| clamped at mass | |
| --- | --- |
| 10 | `wildlifeFor` |
| 26 | `poolFor`'s principal axis |
| 30 | `inheritanceOf` |
| 42 | lattice pitch, suspension density, depth mode |
| 43 | volley cadence |
| 45 | the medium's sound speed |

`maxHp` is `30 + mass * 10` and has **no clamp at all**. So a sixfold throne has
bought everything there is to buy by its **fourth** helping. That report's throne
was fed fifty: **4 bought something, 46 bought 5340 hit points and nothing
else.** A throne stopped where it was sated would have woken 510.

The trap is that overfeeding looks exactly like progress. The panel showed the
cost honestly — hit points, discharges to kill — and one benefit,
`NEXT WORLD KEEPS 60%`, which is a clamped number that reads the same on both
sides of its clamp.

**Shipped:** `nextHelpingBuys` and `throneLedger` in `game/world.ts`, which ask
the world's own functions what would actually change rather than restating the
ceilings, so they move when a ceiling is retuned. The throne panel gains a line
naming what one more helping buys, or `SATED · ANOTHER 622 IS +120 HP AND
NOTHING ELSE` in red. A `sated` lesson. The `crown` lesson, which said "FEED IT
MORE ... THAT TRADE IS THE GAME" with no bottom, now names the bottom. The
diagnosis gained a branch above the discharge advice. Every report now carries
`(4 of 50 bought anything; 5340 hp of nothing)` on its throne line.

**The gate matters.** The same three reports contain the control: the aeon-1
throne was fed seven helpings of which SIX bought something. That player was not
overfeeding, and the new line is gated so it does not accuse them of it.

## 2. `wearing < 6` was a threshold on a sample rate

The diagnosis decided "they never used the hand" with `n("wearing") < 6`.
`wearing` is pushed on a Poisson tick at six a second, so that threshold asked
*"did you hold it for less than one second"*. The three reports read 10, 9 and 8
— a second and a half of contact each, across five, eleven and twenty-three
minutes — and all three were scored as having learned the hand and routed past
the advice about it.

It is now what the hand actually took off the bar:
`ticks / WEAR_TICKS_PER_SECOND * wearRate` against a tenth of `maxHp`. The
constant is exported from `run.ts` and used in both places, so the count and the
thing that reads it cannot drift apart.

**The general lesson, which is in `HANDOFF.md` now: a threshold on an event
count is a threshold on a sample rate. Say which, in the name.**

## 3. Every pasted report accused its author of not clicking the game

All three reports carried:

```
pad: CLICK THE GAME - A PAGE WITHOUT FOCUS GETS NO PAD INPUT
```

from a player who was using the pad the whole time. `pad.describe()` asked about
focus **first**, and `report()` is only ever reached by typing into the browser
console — which is exactly when the page does not have focus. The one line in a
report that describes the controller was guaranteed to be wrong in the only
context a report is ever produced in.

A pad that has ever delivered input has answered the focus question by
demonstration. It now says what the pad *is*, and marks the axes stale.

## 4. `doFeed`'s message has never been on screen

Found while fixing (1). `act()` runs before `step()` and `drain()`, so the toast
`doFeed` wrote — `FED n · 622 · 5 DISCHARGES TO KILL`, the one actionable number
at the moment of the decision — was overwritten by `drain`'s
`THE THRONE TAKES 622 · C TO CROWN` in the same frame, every time.

This is the same shape as the `teach()` clobbering bug already in
`HANDOFF.md`, where the `aim` lesson had never been shown to anybody ever.
**A toast written before `drain` is a toast that was never shown.** Everything
the feed says now lives in the `fed` event handler, which runs last.

## 5. A refusal was about the neighbourhood, not the pair

`mergePass` took the nearest partner inside `BIND_RADIUS`, tried it, and gave up
for the frame if it would not join. The rule is *"a pentamer joins nothing,
ever"* — the crystallographic restriction theorem, spent on purpose. The code
was saying something stronger and nobody had written it down: a pentamer one
micron nearer stopped a girdle eighteen microns away from binding too.

**Measured, and then measured again the other way, which is the part worth
keeping.** The shadowing is real: 2.9 per cent of binding opportunities lost in
the first water, 8 per cent from the third on, a quarter of every refusal
holding a partner in reach that was never asked. **And it is worth no cells:**
A/B over four seeds and three waters, 240 s each, gave 1106 → 1096, 826 → 829,
1178 → 1183. Noise.

It is kept because the code now says what the rules say, and because it costs
nothing — it runs only on a refusal and allocates nothing. It is *documented* as
worth nothing so that a future session does not go looking for gathering wins
here.

## How to check any of this

Every number above came from a probe that prints its own reasons. They are not
in the repo (they were scratch), but they are cheap to rebuild — each one is
twenty lines that imports `game/` and prints a table. The two that mattered:

- feed a throne one cell at a time and diff `worldFrom` either side of each
  helping; print which named quantity moved. That table *is* finding (1).
- walk `mergePass`'s own nine-square search over a settled pool and count the
  motifs whose nearest partner refuses while a joinable one stands in reach.
  That number is finding (5), and the A/B that followed is why finding (5) is
  documented as worthless rather than sold as a win.
